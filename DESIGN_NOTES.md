# Portfolio design principles

## Agreed direction

- A one-screen entrance built around Daan van Vugt’s name in sculpted blue type.
- One row: Daan van Vugt. Selected typeface: Space Grotesk Bold. Solid satin faces, bevelled edges, soft shadows.
- Selected movement: Bouncy, with slow idle movement enabled. Local sideways/vertical travel, tilt, and a press/spring return; letters always return to their positions.
- The chosen comparison is `/workshop?motion=bouncy&font=space&idle=1`. Keep the workshop available to compare fonts and movement without changing the main default.
- White and blue as the primary reference; retain the existing light/dark/system preference.
- Clear role and experience facts, with an immediately available link to the overview.

## Checklist for future changes

1. Text must communicate a fact, explain something, or give a useful instruction. Use project names and straightforward section labels. Remove filler slogans and redundant captions.
2. Give each page one dominant visual idea. On the entrance, that is the name; on the overview, the project imagery carries the work.
3. Make identity and navigation clear from the first screen. A visitor never has to complete an animation or discover a hidden interaction to enter the portfolio.
4. Let interaction reward exploration. Keep the name readable and navigation usable during movement, while loading, and if graphics fail.
5. Keep touch, keyboard, reduced-motion, small-screen, and short-screen experiences usable. Decorative interactions do not become keyboard obstacles.
6. Preserve the distinction between a product’s capabilities, Daan’s contributions, conceptual illustrations, and inherited work.

## References and observations

- [Figma’s portfolio examples](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/portfolio-website-examples/): curated work, clear roles and context, and distinct visual approaches. The live sites were inspected as well as the article.
- [Dion Pieters](https://www.dionpieters.dev/): the name provides the visual scale, supported by concise factual labels. Borrow the hierarchy, not its colours or effects.
- [Karina Sirqueira](https://karinasirqueira.com/): one strong composition gives the entrance a memorable identity. Keep explicit navigation in our version.
- [Mike Matas](https://mikematas.com/): large actual product previews communicate the work directly. Retain this principle in the overview and future case studies.
- [Shadcn Studio Grid 14](https://shadcnstudio.com/blocks/bento-grid/bento-grid): the existing overview’s card layout, illustrative treatment, and focused motion remain useful.
